Who was Alfred Nobel?

Alfred Nobel (1833–1896) was a Swedish inventor and industrialist best known for inventing dynamite. In his will he dedicated a large portion of his estate to fund annual prizes for those who have conferred the greatest benefit on humanity in the fields of physics, chemistry, medicine, literature, and peace.

Image: Alfred Nobel

What is the Peace Prize?

The Nobel Peace Prize is awarded to individuals or organizations that have made outstanding efforts to promote peace, resolve conflicts, protect human rights, or disarm. The prize is awarded annually by the Norwegian Nobel Committee according to the terms of Alfred Nobel's will.

Nobel Peace Prize Winners — Last 10 Years (2015–2024)

A brief list with a short note about each laureate.

2015 — Tunisian National Dialogue Quartet

A group of four Tunisian organizations that played a decisive role in supporting the peaceful transition to democracy after the Jasmine Revolution.

2016 — Juan Manuel Santos (Colombia)

The president of Colombia who led peace negotiations to end a conflict of more than five decades with the FARC group.

2017 — International Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons (ICAN)

A global movement that drew attention to the humanitarian consequences of nuclear weapons and promoted a treaty to prohibit them.

2018 — Denis Mukwege & Nadia Murad

Denis: a Congolese doctor who has treated victims of sexual violence. Nadia: a Yazidi activist who survived captivity by ISIS and has brought attention to sexual violence crimes.

2019 — Abiy Ahmed Ali (Ethiopia)

Prime Minister of Ethiopia who led initiatives for peace and regional cooperation, notably with Eritrea.

2020 — World Food Programme (WFP)

An international humanitarian organization that fought hunger and contributed to stability and peace in conflict areas.

2021 — Maria Ressa & Dmitry Muratov

Two journalists who defended freedom of expression and independent media as a cornerstone of democracy and peace.

2022 — Ales Bialiatski, Memorial and Center for Civil Liberties

The prize recognized civil society that documented human rights abuses and defended fundamental rights in their countries.

2023 — Narges Mohammadi (Iran)

An Iranian human rights activist who campaigned for women's rights and fundamental freedoms inside Iran.

2024 — Nihon Hidankyo (Association of Atomic Bomb Victims in Japan)

An organization representing survivors of atomic and hydrogen bombs in Japan, reminding the world of the horrors of nuclear weapons and campaigning for their abolition.

Nominees for the Upcoming Year — Important Note

The official list of Nobel nominees is confidential for 50 years and is not published. Therefore, an "official" list of next-year nominees cannot be provided. However, media outlets sometimes circulate names and organizations as potential candidates based on their work or impact.

Nelson Mandela — His Story

Nelson Mandela (1918–2013) was a leader of the struggle against apartheid in South Africa, imprisoned for 27 years, then released to negotiate the end of apartheid and become South Africa's first Black president (1994–1999). He won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1993 together with F. W. de Klerk for their joint efforts to end apartheid and build national reconciliation.

Brief story: Mandela led the African National Congress against apartheid policies. His suffering, stance, and vision for reconciliation gave him global credibility. After many years in prison he promoted national reconciliation instead of revenge, leaving a legacy of dignity and human rights.

Impact of the Nobel Peace Prize Worldwide

The Nobel Peace Prize highlights important humanitarian and political issues, giving laureates an international platform to raise awareness, attract support and influence policy, and mobilize resources. Some laureates used the prize to accelerate peace negotiations, protect vulnerable groups, or defend freedom of expression and civil institutions. Its impact, however, remains dependent on political context and some awards have sparked debate over timing or effectiveness.
